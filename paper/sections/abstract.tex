\begin{abstract}
Quantum reservoir computing usually compresses repeated measurements into an
expectation-value vector for each time step. We introduce adaptive shot aggregation: a
fixed measurement record is partitioned into disjoint groups and averaged
within each group. This spans individual shots and full averaging without additional quantum
executions. We evaluate the method on Mackey--Glass, Santa Fe laser, R\"ossler,
and sunspots under a shared simulation protocol and fixed execution budgets.
At the balanced budget, grouping lowers mean test NRMSE across every task
relative to full averaging. Santa Fe shows the strongest improvement, with
every test window favoring grouping. A control that applies the observed joint
shot fluctuations directly to the averaged features performs similarly,
indicating that grouping mainly regularizes the classical readout.
Unmitigated experiments with a separate hardware circuit on \emph{ibm\_fez}
and \emph{ibm\_marrakesh} show the same positive trend across every task. Shot
aggregation therefore offers a practical way to improve finite-shot learning
at no added quantum cost.
\end{abstract}
