\subsection{Why grouping regularizes the readout}
\label{sec:mechanism}

Full averaging hands the readout one clean picture of each time step.
Individual shots hand it many pictures, each blurry. Intermediate groups hand
it a few lightly cleaned pictures of the same target, and the differences
between those pictures reveal which parts of the feature vector are stable and
which are only measurement noise. Fitting the readout to all of the views at
once discourages it from trusting the unstable parts.

For a linear readout with squared loss this becomes an exact identity. Let
$\bar{x}_t$ be the average of the $G$ grouped views, and let
$C_{k,t}=\tfrac{1}{G}\sum_g
(x_t^{(g)}-\bar{x}_t)(x_t^{(g)}-\bar{x}_t)^\top$
record how those views scatter around their average, that is, how the features
wobble together from group to group. For any intercept $b$ and weights $w$,
\begin{equation}
  \frac{1}{G}\sum_g \bigl(y_t-b-w^\top x_t^{(g)}\bigr)^2
  =\bigl(y_t-b-w^\top\bar{x}_t\bigr)^2+w^\top C_{k,t}\,w.
  \label{eq:loss-decomposition}
\end{equation}
The first term on the right is exactly the EV loss. The second charges extra
whenever the weights lean on feature combinations that vary strongly across
the views. Grouped training is therefore EV training plus a penalty read
directly off the observed shot fluctuations, and the identity assumes neither
Gaussian noise nor independence over time.

The identity motivates a matched baseline: covariance-regularized
expectation-value training (Cov-EV). Cov-EV keeps the fully averaged EV
features and adds a penalty estimated from how the individual shots fluctuate
together. Its strength and the ordinary ridge strength are chosen using the
same earlier data as the other methods. Cov-EV does not reuse the aggregation
scale chosen by Grouped; it tests whether the same regularization mechanism can
be applied directly after full averaging. Similar performance from Grouped and
Cov-EV would therefore support regularization as the source of the gain.

The equivalence is also verified computationally. When $k$ is fixed, Grouped
training and EV training with the penalty defined by $C_{k,t}$ give identical
predictions. Because the leaky integrator and overlapping lags make
neighboring feature vectors dependent, we report uncertainty across
trajectories or measured windows rather than across the many correlated
grouped views.
