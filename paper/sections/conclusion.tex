\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

Adaptive shot aggregation changes only the classical use of a measurement
record. The aggregation scale $k$ determines how many shots form each training
view, while Raw and EV remain available as its two endpoints. Earlier time
blocks choose $k$ and the ridge strength before the test block is evaluated.

The method requires no additional circuit executions and is most useful when a
record contains many shots per time step but relatively few time steps. In this
regime, partial averaging supplies several denoised views of each target.
Equation~\eqref{eq:loss-decomposition} shows that these views impose a penalty
derived from the measured shot fluctuations. Cov-EV obtains slightly lower
mean errors but remains close to Grouped, supporting covariance regularization
as the mechanism while showing that grouping is not its only implementation.

The positive trend transfers from the six-qubit simulation reservoir to a
separate ten-qubit circuit on two processors. This comparison tests transfer of
the aggregation procedure, not the effect of qubit count. Alternative scales
require individual measurement outcomes and cannot be recovered from stored
expectation values. The hardware intervals describe blocks from one acquisition
per task and processor; independent campaigns and other reservoir families are
the next tests.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Adaptive shot aggregation turns a fixed measurement record into training views
at a scale suited to the task. Intermediate aggregation reduces mean test error
on all four tasks at the balanced budget, and both processors reproduce the
positive trend. Retaining shot-level records therefore enables a practical
post-execution improvement without changing the circuit or increasing the
quantum execution budget.

\appendices
\section{Reproducibility Summary}
\label{app:reproducibility}

All simulation tasks use the same six-qubit reservoir, preprocessing,
chronological selection procedure, and execution budgets. Mackey--Glass and
R\"ossler use five trajectories; Santa Fe and sunspots use five windows with
disjoint test blocks. Repeated settings are averaged within each unit before
uncertainty is computed. All methods share the raw record and ridge candidates.
The three budgets are $(T,N)=(250,40),(500,20),(1000,10)$, each totaling
10,000 executions. Hardware uses one fixed ten-qubit circuit and one 400-shot
record per task and processor, split into twenty disjoint $N=20$ blocks. The
supplement and \ifdefined\ANON artifact\else
\href{https://github.com/eybmits/split-ensemble-qrc}{artifact}\fi provide all
splits, settings, outputs, hardware job IDs, checksums, and rebuild scripts.
