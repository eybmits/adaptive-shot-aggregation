\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

Measurement grouping has prior use. Cemin \emph{et al.} average subsets of
measurements to enlarge a training set for reduced quantum channel learning
~\cite{cemin2024channels}, and training with noisy inputs is related to
an additional penalty on large fitted weights~\cite{bishop1995training}. This study evaluates the
complete range from Raw to EV on a fixed QRC record. It adds joint calibration
of aggregation and readout regularization, an exact explanation for the
resulting penalty, three
allocations of a fixed execution budget, and a matched evaluation on four
forecasting tasks. The contribution is the calibration and evaluation of the
aggregation scale, not the original introduction of subset averaging.

Finite sampling has also been addressed at other stages. Ahmed \emph{et al.}
derive noise-aware training after expectation values have been formed
~\cite{ahmed2025optimal}. Kreplin and Roth use variance regularization to reduce
sampling noise~\cite{kreplin2024reduction}, while resource-aware optimization
allocates measurements before execution~\cite{moussa2023resource}. Sampling
limits and useful combinations of features have also been studied in physical
learning systems~\cite{hu2023tackling}. Our method begins after execution and
uses a single archived measurement record.

QRC research has examined how noise, dissipation, measurement, finite Hilbert
space, and input transformations shape temporal features
~\cite{kubota2022noise,palacios2024role,kubota2023temporal,martinezpena2023finite,mujal2023timeseries,suzuki2022natural,sannia2024dissipation,govia2022nonlinear,martinezpena2023capacity,kalfus2022hilbert,fry2023noiseinduced,govia2021singleoscillator}.
Those studies analyze or modify the quantum representation. Here the
representation is held fixed and only the organization of repeated
measurements changes.
