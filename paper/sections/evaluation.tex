\section{Evaluation}
\label{sec:results}

\subsection{Balanced setting}

Table~\ref{tab:balanced} and Fig.~\ref{fig:operating} summarize the balanced
setting. Grouped lowers mean NRMSE relative to EV by \QAIMackeyEVGrouped{} on Mackey--Glass
(95\% CI $[\QAIMackeyEVGroupedLow,\QAIMackeyEVGroupedHigh]$),
\QAISantaFeEVGrouped{} on Santa Fe laser
($[\QAISantaFeEVGroupedLow,\QAISantaFeEVGroupedHigh]$),
\QAIRosslerEVGrouped{} on R\"ossler
($[\QAIRosslerEVGroupedLow,\QAIRosslerEVGroupedHigh]$), and
\QAISunspotsEVGrouped{} on sunspots
($[\QAISunspotsEVGroupedLow,\QAISunspotsEVGroupedHigh]$). The intervals exclude
zero for both real-world datasets and R\"ossler. The interval for Mackey--Glass
includes zero.

Santa Fe has the largest difference at the balanced setting. Adaptive shot
aggregation reduces EV from 0.764 to 0.680. Each of the five disjoint windows
favors Grouped. Joint tuning favors $k=10$ most often and an intermediate
aggregation scale in 94\% of repeated trials. Cov-EV reaches 0.677, nearly
matching Grouped, while the Grouped gain decreases from 0.143 with 40 shots per
step to 0.010 with 10 shots per step. This pattern is consistent with
grouping changing the readout differently for different tasks and execution
allocations.

The ideal QRC model performs worse than the identically processed classical
input on Mackey--Glass and sunspots, better on Santa Fe, and yields an
inconclusive difference on R\"ossler. The experiments therefore support claims
about aggregation of a chosen reservoir record, but not quantum advantage.

The chosen aggregation scale varies across tasks, trajectories, circuit
parameter settings, measurement samples, and grouping assignments. EV is
preferred in some cases.
Equation~\eqref{eq:covariance} shows that changing $k$ changes which feature
combinations the readout is discouraged from using, not only how strongly it
is discouraged. Raw generally has higher error, while
several intermediate aggregation scales remain competitive with EV.

\subsection{Time steps and shots}

Figure~\ref{fig:regimes} compares three allocations of the same 10,000
executions. With 40 shots at each of 250 time steps, every task has a positive
mean difference between 0.086 and 0.170. The intervals exclude zero for
Mackey--Glass, Santa Fe, and R\"ossler, while the sunspot result varies across
windows. With 10 shots at each of 1,000 time steps, the mean differences fall
between 0.008 and 0.016. The intervals exclude zero for Mackey--Glass and
sunspots and include zero for Santa Fe and R\"ossler. Grouping has its largest
effect when the record contains many shots per time step and fewer time steps.

\subsection{Controls and grouping robustness}

Cov-EV has a lower mean than Grouped on all four tasks at the balanced setting.
The paired differences range from 0.0021 to 0.0116 NRMSE, and only the
R\"ossler interval excludes zero. Grouped obtains its fluctuation-based penalty
directly from the record without introducing another adjustable strength. Cov-EV applies
the same fluctuation-based penalty directly to EV and chooses its strength from
earlier data. Their similar performance supports the explanation in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:covariance}.

Raw has higher error than EV on Mackey--Glass and R\"ossler at the balanced
setting. The intervals for Santa Fe and sunspots include zero. The exact
two-form calculation matches Grouped ridge at fixed $k$ to numerical precision
and verifies the implementation. It differs from Cov-EV, which chooses the
strength of the fluctuation-based penalty independently. Across twenty repeated
settings in the sensitivity analysis, EV minus Grouped means are $-0.0064$
on Mackey--Glass, $0.1103$ on Santa Fe, $0.0177$ on R\"ossler, and $0.0493$ on
sunspots; only the sunspot interval excludes zero. Fixed-group minus
per-time-regrouped mean differences are at most 0.0021 NRMSE in magnitude and
every clustered interval contains zero. The grouping convention therefore has
little effect relative to variation across repeated settings. Figure~\ref{fig:controls}
summarizes the matched controls for all four tasks.

\subsection{Hardware transfer}

All four tasks have positive mean EV minus Grouped differences on both processors
(Fig.~\ref{fig:hardware}): 0.038--0.153 NRMSE on \emph{ibm\_fez} and
0.031--0.103 on \emph{ibm\_marrakesh}. For Santa Fe, EV decreases from
\QAIHardwareFezSantaEV{} to \QAIHardwareFezSantaGrouped{} on Fez, a gain of
\QAIHardwareFezSantaGain{} ($[\QAIHardwareFezSantaGainLow,
\QAIHardwareFezSantaGainHigh]$), and from \QAIHardwareMarrakeshSantaEV{} to
\QAIHardwareMarrakeshSantaGrouped{} on Marrakesh, a gain of
\QAIHardwareMarrakeshSantaGain{} ($[\QAIHardwareMarrakeshSantaGainLow,
\QAIHardwareMarrakeshSantaGainHigh]$). Grouped wins \QAIHardwareSantaWins{}
blocks on each processor. Santa Fe was acquired later using the same circuit,
physical layouts, and analysis. These paired intervals are descriptive of
variation among measurement blocks within one acquisition. They do not measure
variation across independent hardware campaigns.
